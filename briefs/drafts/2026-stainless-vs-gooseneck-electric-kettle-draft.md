### Intro

Apartment kitchens do not need two kettles. They need one that matches the job.

If you mostly make tea, instant oats, or guest mugs, a wide-spout stainless kettle is the boring win. If pour-over coffee is the daily ritual, a gooseneck’s thin spout is the upgrade that actually changes the cup.

This short vs guide compares those two shapes only — capacity, pour control, and who should skip each. Specs and owner complaint themes, not a kitchen lab. More kitchen picks live in [Home & Kitchen](https://pickora.shop/home-kitchen/). Espresso buyers can jump to our [espresso machine guide](https://pickora.shop/how-to-choose-an-espresso-machine/).

### FAQ

**Do I need a gooseneck if I only drink tea?**  
Usually no. A stainless everyday kettle is enough unless you care about exact temperatures for green or white tea.

**Is variable temperature worth it?**  
Yes if you brew pour-over or delicate teas. No if you only need a rolling boil.

**How often should I descale?**  
In hard-water cities, many owners wipe scale monthly and run a vinegar or citric cycle when the kettle looks chalky or boils loudly.

### Verdict

Get the **stainless kettle** if hot water is a utility — tea, guests, instant food. Get the **gooseneck** if coffee pour-over is the reason you own a kettle. Owning both is rare; pick the spout you will use every morning.
